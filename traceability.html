Certification

What the organic claim rests on

No laboratory can look at a bale of fibre and tell you whether it was grown organically - organic and conventional fibre are identical under a microscope. That is what certification is for, and this page sets out exactly what ours covers.

The certificates

Two levels, held by two parties

Organic certification of cotton always works in two layers, and it is worth knowing which is which before you buy from anyone.

At the farm

The land and the crop are certified organic under NPOP and NOP, held by the farms we contract and inspected annually by an accredited certification body. That certificate is what makes the cotton organic in the first place.

We do not issue it, we cannot influence it, and if it lapses we cannot sell the crop as organic.

At the mill

Our own GOTS and OCS scope certificates cover cleaning, baling and the sale of certified organic fibre. They confirm that what went into the mill as certified organic picked cotton came out as certified organic cleaned fibre, without being mixed with anything else.

A transaction certificate is then issued for your specific shipment. That is the document your auditor will actually ask for.

Testing

What we measure, and how often

Certification is a paper system. Testing is what stops it drifting away from the fibre. We run both, and we send you the results either way.

  • Bench test on every lot - bulk density, loft recovery, seed and shell, dust and colour, from our own bench.
  • Moisture at intake and again at despatch.
  • Restricted substance screening, every season, at an accredited external laboratory.
  • Fibre identification confirming the species, every season.
  • Pesticide residue screening, annually.
  • Organic inspection of the contracted land, annually, by the certification body.

Why we test for identity

The commonest way a certified lot goes wrong is not fraud at the farm. It is cheaper fibre being blended in somewhere between the grove and the bale.

Fibre identification every season is what catches that. It is a check on what is actually in the lot rather than on anybody's paperwork, and together with the volume reconciliation below it is the most informative thing we do.

Check the certificate, not the company. Our scope certificate numbers are verifiable on the certifier's public database. Confirm ours before you place a first order - we would rather you did.

What you are buying

The claim has to survive the bale.

Certification covers the land and the mill. Testing is what confirms that the fibre in your consignment is the fibre the certificate describes.

Documents

What ships with the cotton

None of this is issued on request or at extra cost. It is the standard pack, and if any of it is missing from your consignment that is a mistake worth telling us about.

  • Test report for the lot you bought.
  • Transaction certificate naming us as seller, you as buyer, and the certified organic status.
  • Weighbridge slip and bale list with net weight per bale.
  • Copies of our scope certificates, valid on the date of despatch.
  • Seasonal test reports - fibre identification, residue and restricted substance screening.
  • Phytosanitary and fumigation-free declaration where your import rules need one.
Volume

The check that catches overselling

Our certifier compares the volume of certified organic fibre we sell against what our contracted acreage can plausibly yield. Overselling is the most common failure mode in organic cotton, and reconciliation is what catches it.

Yields per hectare from a perennial grove are lower than from an annual field crop, so a volume claim that would be unremarkable elsewhere can be arithmetically impossible here. That is why we publish our acreage and our seasonal tonnage - the arithmetic is there for anyone who wants to do it.

Limits

What certification does not prove

A valid certificate proves the land was inspected and the mill was audited. It does not make the fibre something it is not, and it does not settle every question a buyer might reasonably ask.

Two claims we will not make: that hand-picking guarantees no child ever worked on a contracted farm - our quarterly checks reduce that risk, they do not eliminate it - and that a rain-fed perennial crop has no water footprint at all. It has a small one. Ask us for the numbers rather than the adjectives.

We also cannot certify anything that happens after the cotton leaves us. Once it is spun, woven or made up by somebody else, the organic claim depends on their certificate, not ours.
