Who we are

A gin that starts in the field

Most organic lint reaches a spinner through a trader who bought it from a gin that bought it at market. We contract the crop before the season and gin it ourselves, so there is nobody in between to take a margin or lose the paperwork.

The crop is Gossypium arboreum — a woody perennial that stands for several seasons rather than being ploughed in and resown every year. It is grown rain-fed on marginal ground and picked by hand over three to five passes as the bolls open.

Then it goes across our weighbridge, into the drying yard and onto the roller gins. What comes out is baled, tested and sold. We are not a mill and we have no ambition to become one.

The fibre

Know what you are buying

Tree cotton is not a cheap substitute for upland cotton. It is a different fibre — shorter, coarser and far more absorbent — and it rewards being specified deliberately.

  • Absorbency — wets out fast and holds water; the traditional fibre for surgical cotton, towelling and wadding.
  • Coarse counts — spins comfortably to about Ne 24s. Above that, buy something else.
  • Character — visible texture and slub, which is the point in khadi, denim weft and rustic wovens.
  • Non-GM by species — there are no commercial GM varieties of G. arboreum.
  • Perennial and rain-fed — no annual replanting, no irrigation drawn on our acreage.

Why buy from us

What you get that a lint trader cannot offer

Straight from the gin

We contracted the crop, we ginned it, and we are selling it. One counterparty, one set of documents, and one place to call when something is wrong.

Measured, not described

HVI on every lot from our own lab, plus seasonal GM screening and species identification from an accredited external laboratory.

Priced from the farm gate

We publish the premium paid to growers each season. No broker layer means your cost is the cotton, the ginning and our margin — nothing else.

Roller-ginned, always
