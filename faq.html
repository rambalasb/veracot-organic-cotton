Buyer FAQ

The questions we get asked before a first order

Quantities, pricing, certification and the things tree cotton genuinely cannot do. If your question is not here, the sales desk answers email faster than it answers the phone.

Ordering

Placing an order

What is your minimum order quantity?

14 MT, which is one 20 ft container or one truckload. Below that a lint trader is a better route than a gin.

How long does an order take?

7–14 days in season, November to April. Out of season we ship from warehouse stock, usually within three weeks. Sea freight on top of that depends on your destination — we confirm it with the quote.

What do I see before I commit?

The HVI report for the lot we are offering, so what you are quoted is a measurement rather than a description. Independent re-testing at your own laboratory is welcome at any point.

Who normally buys from you?

Spinning mills, absorbent-cotton makers, khadi institutions and converters — anyone who processes raw fibre. We also sell to a handful of brands who buy the lint and place it with their own nominated mill.

Is the fibre available all year?

Ginning runs November to April. We warehouse lint and ship year-round, but the best price and the widest availability are in season. Forward contracts against the coming crop open in July.

Price and terms

Money

Why is there no price list?

Cotton is an agricultural product and its cost moves with the season. We price each lot against the actual farm-gate cost plus a published ginning charge, so a list published in June would be wrong by December. Ask and you will get a firm price valid for 30 days.

How does organic tree cotton compare on price to conventional cotton?

Expect a premium. Lower yields per hectare, hand-picking, roller ginning and a farm-gate premium to the grower all cost money. Against certified organic upland cotton the gap is much narrower, and at coarse counts tree cotton is often the cheaper of the two.

What payment terms do you offer?

New customers: 30% advance with the order, balance against scanned documents. Once we have a track record together: irrevocable LC at sight, or 30-day open credit against a signed contract.

Which Incoterms do you work on?

Ex works at the gin, FOB, or CIF to your named port. We quote all three so you can compare against your own freight rates. Domestic Indian orders are delivered door to door.

Do you hold stock?

Yes. Baled lint is warehoused at the gin through the year, which is how we ship out of season. Stock moves within the season — we do not carry cotton over and sell it as new crop.

Quality and claims

Certification, testing and what you may say on a label

Which certifications do you hold?

GOTS and OCS scope certificates covering ginning, baling and the sale of certified organic lint; NPOP/NOP organic certification at farm level on the contracted acreage; Fairtrade cotton on about 40% of it; and a SEDEX SMETA social audit at the gin. Details here. We do not hold OEKO-TEX — that belongs on finished textiles, and we do not make any.

Can I put "organic" on my label?

Only if the transaction certificate for your specific lot supports it, and only in the form your own market's rules allow. We issue that certificate with every order. Note that the claim has to be carried forward by your own spinner's and weaver's certificates too — ours covers the fibre up to the point it left us, and no further.

Is tree cotton GM-free?

There are no commercially released genetically modified varieties of Gossypium arboreum, so the species is non-GM by identity. We still run a PCR screen every season, because a positive result would indicate upland cotton contamination rather than modified tree cotton — which is exactly the thing worth catching.

What test data comes with a shipment?

A full HVI report for your lot — length, uniformity, strength, micronaire, trash and colour — plus moisture at despatch. The season's external laboratory reports for GM screening, fibre identification and residue screening travel with it, passed through unedited.

How do I raise a quality claim?

Within 30 days of arrival, with the lot number and your own test results. We keep retained material from every lot and re-test it against your complaint. If we are wrong we replace or credit the cotton; if the retained material is in specification we will tell you that too.

The fibre

What tree cotton can and cannot do

How fine a count can I spin from it?

To about Ne 24s on a ring frame, and that needs a good lot. If you need Ne 40s and above, tree cotton is the wrong fibre and we will say so rather than sell you cotton that will not run on your frames.

Is it soft enough for next-to-skin knitwear?

100% tree cotton knits have a dry, textured handle that some buyers specifically want and others find too rustic. Most customers making next-to-skin knitwear blend it 30/70 or 50/50 with organic upland cotton. This is a handle question rather than a data one, so read it against your own end product.

How consistent is it batch to batch?

Less consistent than machine-harvested annual cotton. It is a hand-picked perennial crop from smallholder groves, and micronaire in particular moves with the season. Buy against a range rather than a single point, and read the HVI report for the lot rather than the averages on this site.

What is it genuinely best at?

Absorbency and texture. Surgical and absorbent cotton, wadding, towelling, muslin, khadi and handloom cloth, denim weft, and any construction where visible irregularity is the point rather than a defect.

Is this the same as kapok or silk cotton?

No. Tree cotton is true cotton — seed-hair fibre from a Gossypium boll, ginned and spun in the normal way. Kapok comes from Ceiba and Bombax trees, has hollow fibres, and is used as filling rather than spun. More on the botany.

Logistics and visits

Getting it to you

How does it ship?

By sea in containers loaded at the gin for export orders, and by road for domestic ones. Freight is quoted with the offer once we know the destination.

Can I send a third-party inspector?

Yes, at your cost. Bales can be drawn at random and sent for independent HVI testing. We also send the lot data before every despatch as standard.

Can I visit the gin and the groves?

Please do. December to March is the useful window — the gin is running and the groves are still in picking, so you can see the crop, the gin line and the press in a day. Two weeks' notice and we will send directions and arrange transport. Arrange a visit.

Do you work through agents?

We deal direct wherever we can — it is how we keep the farm-gate premium high and the price to you sensible. If you already work with an agent in India we will honour that relationship, but the quote will look the same either way.

Still have a question?

Ask it directly. A real person at the mill office reads the enquiry form and the sales inbox, and you will hear back within two working days.
